# Power BI

Duration: 4 months / 16 teaching weeks.
Schedule: 6 instructor-led hours weekly (96 total), plus 4 to 6 independent study hours.

Design business intelligence reports from source preparation through semantic modeling, DAX calculations, visual design, and controlled publishing. Students learn to make reports accurate, interpretable, and maintainable.

## Prerequisites

Spreadsheet familiarity; introductory SQL recommended.

## Tools

Power BI Desktop on a supported Windows environment; sample datasets; optional Power BI service access.

## Learning outcomes

- Transform source data with Power Query and document quality checks.
- Build a star schema with deliberate relationships and a date table.
- Develop DAX measures and explain filter context.
- Design accessible reports and configure appropriate sharing, refresh, and security.

## Weekly schedule

### Week 1 — BI foundations

Business questions, KPIs, report audiences, Desktop navigation.

Practical: Translate a stakeholder brief into report requirements.

### Week 2 — Data connections

Excel, CSV, database sources, import and DirectQuery concepts.

Practical: Connect to sales sources and document refresh assumptions.

### Week 3 — Power Query essentials

Types, filtering, splitting, replacements, missing values.

Practical: Create a repeatable customer-cleaning workflow.

### Week 4 — Advanced preparation

Merge, append, pivot, unpivot, parameters, query folding concepts.

Practical: Combine monthly files and verify row counts.

### Week 5 — Semantic modeling

Fact and dimension tables, grain, cardinality, filter direction.

Practical: Build a sales star schema and explain relationships.

### Week 6 — DAX foundations

Measures versus columns, SUMX, DIVIDE, variables, context.

Practical: Create revenue, margin, and weighted calculations.

### Week 7 — Time intelligence

Date tables, CALCULATE, prior-period and year-to-date measures.

Practical: Validate period comparisons against independent totals.

### Week 8 — Midpoint practical

Data preparation, model review and KPI reconciliation.

Practical: Deliver a working model with tested core measures.

### Week 9 — Visual design

Chart choice, layout, labels, color, accessible design.

Practical: Build an executive summary page with clear hierarchy.

### Week 10 — Report interaction

Slicers, drill-through, tooltips, bookmarks, navigation.

Practical: Create a guided route from summary to customer detail.

### Week 11 — Analytical reporting

Variance, segmentation, distributions, defensible storytelling.

Practical: Explain sales drivers without unsupported causal claims.

### Week 12 — Service and collaboration

Workspaces, apps, publishing, permissions and license implications.

Practical: Demonstrate a controlled publishing workflow.

### Week 13 — Refresh and security

Gateway concepts, scheduled refresh, row-level security, testing.

Practical: Test role access and produce a refresh checklist.

### Week 14 — Performance and governance

Performance Analyzer, model size, measure optimization, ownership.

Practical: Diagnose slow visuals and document a measured improvement.

### Week 15 — Capstone build

Stakeholder feedback, reconciliation, documentation and usability.

Practical: Resolve review findings and finalize the report.

### Week 16 — Capstone presentation

Business narrative, live walkthrough, security and maintenance.

Practical: Present recommendations supported by verified KPIs.

## Capstone

Executive sales and profitability report

Submit a PBIX file, source-to-model documentation, a measure dictionary, reconciled KPIs, role-security tests, and a report walkthrough. Demonstrate service features in an approved tenant where available.

## Assessment

- Weekly practical work: 25%
- Knowledge checks: 10%
- Midpoint practical: 20%
- Capstone artifact: 35%
- Presentation and defense: 10%
